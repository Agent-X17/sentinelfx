Your revised constraint changes the whole design: with only $50 to $150, the system must be built around **survival, micro-risk, and frequent NO TRADE decisions**, not around income extraction. On a $50 account, a $50 day is a 100% daily return; on $100 it is 50%; on $150 it is 33.3%, while $100/day is 200%, 100%, and 66.7% respectively, so that target cannot be treated as a realistic or guaranteed daily objective and should only be viewed as a distant scaling goal after capital growth and verified edge.[1]

## Small-account reality

A normal standard FX micro-position of 0.01 lot on a 100,000-unit contract is usually too coarse for very small balances, because roughly $0.10 per pip means a 20-pip stop risks about $2 before costs, which is already 4% of a $50 account, 2% of a $100 account, and 1.33% of a $150 account.  HFM’s standard-style accounts all list a 0.01 minimum trade size, but its Cent account changes contract size to 1 lot = 1,000 units with 0.01 minimum lot, which makes the minimum live position far smaller than a normal account and materially better for a tiny balance.  Exness’ Standard Cent account likewise uses cent-lot sizing intended for smaller live exposure than a normal Standard account.[2][3][1]

Using that structure, the safest framework is: maximum safe loss per trade around 1% on Account C, 0.5% to 1% on Account B, and often 0.25% to 0.5% on Account A; daily loss caps around 2% to 3%; weekly loss caps around 4% to 6%; and one position at a time unless margin, spread, and correlation are all exceptionally favorable.  On balances this small, spread and commission are not side details but a primary constraint, because even tight commissions per standard lot become disproportionate once you scale them down to tiny trades and combine them with the fixed friction of stop placement and slippage.[4][1][2]

## Copy-trading architecture

Your copy-trading design should treat every external provider as an **advisory signal**, never as executable truth.  The correct modular flow is `SignalProvider -> SignalNormalizer -> ProviderScorer -> Technical/Macro/Regime checks -> RiskManager veto -> PositionSizer -> ExecutionValidator -> broker`, with expired, duplicated, malformed, stale, or unsupported-symbol signals rejected before scoring.[1]

For provider research, the screening rules should prioritize long live history, enough trades, contained drawdown, transparent statistics, and signs that stops are used; it should penalize short track records, tiny sample sizes, high floating drawdown, martingale, grid recovery, averaging losers, and lot-size escalation after losses.  The follower position must always be recalculated from your equity, stop distance, pip value, and current exposure, so if the provider sends 1.00 lot and your smallest safe size is below the broker minimum, the engine returns NO TRADE rather than forcing bad risk.[1]

Before opening any live account, ask official support these Tanzania-specific questions in writing and keep the transcript: which legal entity will hold my Tanzanian account; regulator and license number for that entity; whether Tanzanian residents are currently accepted; exact minimum trade size and whether cent or equivalent accounts are available; supported deposit and withdrawal methods for Tanzania; whether Visa/Mastercard withdrawals back to Tanzanian-issued cards are supported; whether bank withdrawals to Tanzanian banks are supported; minimum withdrawal and processing time by method; whether profits must first return to the original deposit method; whether there are currency-conversion or withdrawal fees; and whether copy trading is available on the same small-balance account type.[2][1]
